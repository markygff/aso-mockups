EVO: technical evidence

Collected by Foundwork on 25 September 2026 for Steve and Harvey at EVO Home Services Ltd. Sites tested: www.evo-plumbingandheating.co.uk and www.evo-homeservices.co.uk. Every command on this page can be run from any computer to check the result.

1. Two corrections to our email

Perplexity and the 429s. We wrote that Perplexity's crawler was "being turned away by your host, a 429 on three tries". That overstated it. Tested properly today, a single request from any AI crawler, Perplexity included, gets a normal 200. The 429s come from a rate limit that your server applies to AI crawlers as a group: roughly four requests in ten seconds, then a 429 for a few seconds. Our Friday test sent requests quickly enough to trip it. Section 2 has the detail.

ChatGPT and your services. You are right that ChatGPT can identify from public information that EVO provides plumbing, heating and drainage and the areas you cover. It gets that from your page text and from other sites. What our email meant, and should have said plainly, is that none of your pages carries structured data (schema.org JSON-LD) stating it explicitly. That is true of every page we checked, including your service and location pages (section 5). It is an improvement, not a sign that you cannot be found.

So: browsers and Googlebot were not limited at this rate. OpenAI's and Anthropic's crawlers, ChatGPT's own fetcher and Perplexity share one limit. When one of them uses it up, the next AI request of any kind is refused for a few seconds. OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT's search crawler, is not blocked: single requests get 200 (section 2), and it sits under the same shared limit.

Where the 429 comes from. The refusal is the standard nginx page, served by the same server as your normal pages (response header server: nginx; your normal pages also send x-powered-by: PleskLin, a Plesk server on Linux). There is no CDN or third-party firewall in front of the site: no Cloudflare, Sucuri or similar headers in any response. So the rule is in your hosting's nginx configuration. We cannot see that configuration from outside, so we cannot say whether your host added it or it came with the Plesk setup.

6. Sitemap, error pages, canonical tags

  • No sitemap. /sitemap.xml, /sitemap_index.xml and /wp-sitemap.xml all return HTTP 500, and robots.txt names none. Search engines have to find pages by following links.
  • A page that does not exist returns 500, not 404. Any unknown address shows "We're sorry, but something went wrong: Web application could not be started" with status 500. Search engines read a 500 as your server failing, not a missing page.
  • No canonical tag on the homepage. Minor, but it is how a site tells search engines which address is the main one when a page can be reached more than one way.
